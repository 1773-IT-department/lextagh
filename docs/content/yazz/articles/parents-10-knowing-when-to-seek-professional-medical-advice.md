### Know When a Conversation Needs a Healthcare Professional

Parents and caregivers don't need to diagnose menstrual or puberty-related health concerns themselves.

Your role is to notice, listen and seek appropriate professional advice when needed.

Seek Medical Advice When You're Concerned About:

### Very Heavy Menstrual Bleeding

If menstrual bleeding appears unusually heavy, requires very frequent product changes, lasts unusually long, or is accompanied by symptoms such as significant weakness or dizziness, seek professional advice.

### Severe Period Pain

If pain is severe, worsening, doesn't respond to ordinary measures, or repeatedly prevents her from participating in school or normal activities, speak with a healthcare professional.

### Significant Changes in Her Menstrual Pattern

Cycles can be irregular after menstruation first begins. However, persistent or concerning changes should be discussed with a qualified professional.

### Puberty Development

If you're concerned that puberty appears to be beginning unusually early, hasn't started when expected, or her periods haven't begun within the expected stage of development, seek medical guidance rather than comparing her with peers.

### Unusual Vaginal Symptoms

Seek advice when discharge is accompanied by symptoms such as persistent itching, burning, pain, irritation, a strong unusual odour or a significant unexpected change.

### Fainting, Severe Weakness or Feeling Very Unwell

Symptoms that appear serious or urgent should receive prompt medical attention.

### Emotional Wellbeing

Puberty can affect emotions, but persistent or significant changes in mood, behaviour, sleep, school participation or everyday functioning deserve attention and appropriate support.

### Trust What She Tells You

If she repeatedly says:

"Something doesn't feel right,"

listen.

She is learning to understand her body, and taking her concerns seriously helps teach an important lifelong skill: speaking up about her health.

### Yazz® Parent Tip

You don't need to have the diagnosis.

You need to know when it's time to ask someone qualified.

Listen. Observe. Ask. Seek help when needed.
