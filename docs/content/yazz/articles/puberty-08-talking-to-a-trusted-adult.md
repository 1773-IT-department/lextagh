### Some Questions Are Easier When You Don't Face Them Alone

Periods, puberty and body changes can sometimes feel awkward to discuss.

But you deserve reliable information.

A trusted adult can help.

### Who Can Be a Trusted Adult?

Depending on your circumstances, this might be:
- A parent
- A caregiver
- An older responsible family member
- A teacher
- A school counsellor
- A school nurse
- A healthcare professional
- Another responsible adult you know and trust
- How Do I Start the Conversation?

You don't need a perfect opening.

Try something simple:

"I have a question about my period."

"Can we talk about something I'm noticing with my body?"

"I learned something about puberty and I want to understand it better."

"My period started and I need some help."

### What If I Feel Embarrassed?

That's understandable.

You could write your question down first or choose a quiet moment to speak.

Remember, parents, caregivers and other adults went through puberty too.
