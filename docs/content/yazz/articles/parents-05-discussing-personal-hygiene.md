### Turn Hygiene into Education, Not Embarrassment

Puberty can bring increased sweating, body odour, oilier skin, vaginal discharge and menstruation.

A young person may not automatically know how to adjust her personal-care routine.

Teach her without shaming her.

Instead of:

"You smell." Try: "As we grow, our bodies can sweat more. Let's talk about a routine that helps you stay clean and comfortable."

### Build a Simple Routine

Teach appropriate habits around:

### Bathing

Regular bathing helps remove sweat and maintain general cleanliness.

### Underwear

Encourage clean underwear daily and whenever a change is needed.

### Menstrual Hygiene

Teach her to:
- Wash her hands before and after changing a sanitary pad.
- Change pads regularly according to her flow and product instructions.
- Wrap used pads securely.
- Place used pads in an appropriate waste bin.
- Never flush sanitary pads down the toilet.
- Genital Hygiene

Explain the difference between the vulva, the external genital area, and the vagina, the internal canal.

The vagina is self-cleaning. Douching or inserting products for routine cleaning is unnecessary and may disturb its natural environment.

Gentle care of the external genital area is generally sufficient.

### Oral and Skin Care

Puberty is also a good time to reinforce regular dental care and simple, gentle skincare habits.

### Yazz® Parent Tip

Good hygiene shouldn't be taught through embarrassment.

Teach the habit. Explain why it matters. Give her the tools to manage it herself.
