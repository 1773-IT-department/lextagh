"My Period Hurts", Don't Automatically Dismiss It

Period discomfort is common, but that doesn't mean every level of pain should simply be accepted.

Menstrual cramps occur when the uterus contracts during menstruation.

She may describe:
- Cramping or aching in the lower abdomen
- Lower-back discomfort
- Headaches
- Tiredness
- Bloating
- Breast tenderness
- Changes in mood
- Experiences vary considerably.

### How Can You Support Her?

Start by listening.

Ask: "How are you feeling?" rather than immediately telling her what she should be feeling.

Depending on her symptoms, simple measures such as rest, hydration, gentle activity where comfortable and warmth applied appropriately may help some people.

For medication, particularly for a child or young teenager, follow the product directions and seek appropriate guidance from a pharmacist or healthcare professional when needed.

### Don't Normalise Severe Pain

Avoid automatically saying:

"Periods hurt. You'll get used to it."

Pain that is severe, worsening, regularly prevents her from attending school or participating in normal activities, or comes with other concerning symptoms deserves professional medical attention.

### Yazz® Parent Tip

Teach her to describe her symptoms rather than hide them.

Listening to her body is part of menstrual education.
